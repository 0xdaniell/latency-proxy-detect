How it works.

1

Your browser answers.

The server sends your browser 100 small messages. Each carries a random code, so no reply can be sent early. That times the full path, all the way to you.

2

Your address answers.

At the same time, the server pings the IP address you connect from and traces the route inside your open connection, which gets past firewalls and home routers.

3

The paths are compared.

Through a VPN, the address is the VPN server, but your browser is somewhere else. If your browser is more than 50 ms farther away, something sits in between.

What it can’t see.

  • Nearby VPNs. A VPN server within roughly 1,000 km of you usually looks like a direct connection.
  • Remote browsers. A browser running on a remote machine ends the path there.
  • Determined adversaries. Whoever controls the proxy can delay its replies. Treat the result as one signal, not proof.

Method from CalcuLatency by Ramesh, Winter, Korman and Ensafi, USENIX Security 2024. This site is an independent, open-source implementation.

Privacy. Each measurement is logged on the server: your IP address, the timings, the result and your browser’s user agent. No cookies. No third-party scripts. To tell whether a route ends inside your network, the server may look your address up on RIPEstat.
